# Redesign archetypes — distinct skeletons per industry

A redesign must feel built **for this brand and this product category**, not a recolored
template. Color + font swaps are not differentiation. The **skeleton** (grid ratios,
hero type, section shapes, density, card anatomy, signature motif) must change per store.

Use this file as the menu of skeletons. Pick the archetype that fits the store's industry
+ personality, or blend two — then synthesise a store-specific design language (see the
"Design differentiation" step in SKILL.md). Each archetype below changes the BONES, not
just the paint.

> Anti-pattern to avoid: the same 4-col card grid + 5-up "shop by category" tiles + the
> identical cart drawer + the same buy-box, reused across unrelated stores. If two
> different-industry redesigns share the same wireframe, you have not differentiated.

---

## 1. Editorial Lookbook
**For:** apparel, fashion, beauty, lifestyle, premium DTC where the *look* sells.
- **Layout:** asymmetric, magazine-like; full-bleed/edge-to-edge imagery; generous
  whitespace; off-grid hero (image one side, large serif statement the other); 2–3 col
  product grids with tall portrait (3/4 or 2/3) cards.
- **Card anatomy:** big model/lifestyle image first; brand + product name + price quiet
  below, aligned to the image edge (borderless). Swatches on hover.
- **Hero:** lifestyle/editorial image with a short statement headline, minimal CTA.
- **Density:** low — let images breathe. Sharp corners, hairline rules, no heavy borders.
- **Type:** high-contrast display serif (Cormorant/Playfair/Fraunces) + clean grotesk
  (Jost/Inter) for UI.
- **Imagery:** model/flat-lay photography, full-bleed bands, editorial "shop the look".
- **Signature:** an editorial split-banner (Womens/Mens or category), a "the edit" band.
- **Study:** & Other Stories, COS, Aesop, Ganni.

## 2. Hype / Drop
**For:** streetwear, sneakers, collectibles, K-pop/anime merch, limited editions.
- **Layout:** bold, full-bleed, high-energy; dense product walls; countdown/“just dropped”
  rails; badges everywhere; can use dark or saturated backgrounds.
- **Card anatomy:** image + loud price + status badge (Pre-order / Sold out / New / Hot) +
  quick-add; scarcity ("only 3 left"). Square or 4/5 cards, tight gaps.
- **Hero:** big promo/drop banner with a punchy CTA; brand-collab strip.
- **Density:** high, energetic. Rounded or chunky buttons; strong accent color.
- **Type:** bold geometric/grotesk (Sora, Space Grotesk, Archivo) heavy weights, tight.
- **Imagery:** product-on-color, hype photography, stickers/graphics allowed.
- **Signature:** countdown, "drops" rail, collab logos, a playful motif (sticker/flame).
- **Study:** Pop Mart, KITH, Supreme, Gymshark (drops).

## 3. Utility / Spec-first
**For:** used/refurbished/second-hand gear, tools, auto parts, golf, electronics, B2B.
- **Layout:** efficient and information-dense; prominent filters (left rail or top), compare,
  grid/list toggle; PDP leads with a structured spec/condition block.
- **Card anatomy:** image on a neutral pad (consistent), **condition grade + key specs**
  (the deciding facts) on the card, price bold. Compare checkbox.
- **Hero:** product/category-led, not lifestyle; trust ("expert-inspected") + scarcity.
- **Density:** medium-high, technical. Can be dark UI. Tables, badges, monospace-ish nums.
- **Type:** technical sans (Inter, IBM Plex Sans, Sora); numbers prominent.
- **Imagery:** clean product shots framed consistently (contain on neutral); corner watermark only.
- **Signature:** condition grade badge, spec table, "only 1 / one-of-a-kind", inspection seal.
- **Study:** Back Market, 2nd Swing Golf, Newegg, Reverb.

## 4. Warm Catalogue
**For:** home & décor, florals, crafts, gifting, food, garden, hobby.
- **Layout:** friendly dense grid (4-col ok), category-first, warm surfaces (cream/kraft);
  approachable banners; volume/bundle messaging.
- **Card anatomy:** product on warm neutral pad, bold readable price, era/material/size cue,
  hover quick-add. Uniform tiles so a big catalogue reads tidy.
- **Hero:** seasonal/offer hero with a clear CTA; "shop by category" tiles are welcome here.
- **Density:** medium-high but warm and tidy, not clinical. Soft corners, kraft/paper tones.
- **Type:** warm serif (Fraunces/Cormorant) + friendly sans (DM Sans/Inter).
- **Imagery:** clean product on neutral/kraft; lifestyle vignettes; flat-lays.
- **Signature:** kraft/paper texture, volume-discount band, category tiles, botanical/leaf motif.
- **Study:** Afloral, Crate & Barrel, Cox & Cox, Etsy category pages.

## 5. Heritage / Boutique
**For:** antiques, fine jewelry, watches, artisanal, single-curator, luxury niche.
- **Layout:** slow, museum-like, lots of whitespace; centered or single-column moments;
  framed media; story-led sections; restrained palette.
- **Card anatomy:** framed product image, serif title, understated price; condition/provenance
  as refined detail. Few items per row (2–3), large.
- **Hero:** atmospheric image + a single elegant line + one quiet CTA (scrim for legibility).
- **Density:** low. Hairlines, serif, generous margins; no badges shouting.
- **Type:** refined serif display (Cormorant/EB Garamond) + quiet sans.
- **Imagery:** styled, gallery-like, neutral backgrounds; a brand monogram watermark.
- **Signature:** monogram/fleuron, provenance/condition note, framed media, "1 of a kind".
- **Study:** 1stDibs, Manufactum, Astier de Villatte, Vestiaire (premium).

## 6. Marketplace / Filter-first
**For:** large multi-brand catalogues, outlets, marketplaces, big SKU counts.
- **Layout:** discovery-first; persistent left filter rail (or sticky top facets), density
  toggle, sort, breadcrumbs, pagination; scannable cards; brand-led entry points.
- **Card anatomy:** image + brand + name + sale price (strikethrough RRP) + swatches +
  hover size-availability/quick-add. Optimised for fast scanning across hundreds of items.
- **Hero:** offer/brand hero, then straight into filters + grid (don't bury the catalogue).
- **Density:** high but organised; neutral surfaces; clear hierarchy.
- **Type:** neutral, legible (Inter/Jost); brand label is a deliberate element.
- **Imagery:** model/product photography, consistent ratios.
- **Signature:** brand strip, faceted filter rail, sale badges, "X products" count.
- **Study:** ASOS, Vinterior, Zalando, Mango Outlet.

## 7. Bold DTC / Conversion
**For:** single-brand DTC, supplements, gadgets, wellness, problem-solution products.
- **Layout:** narrative landing energy; benefit-led hero with strong value prop + primary CTA;
  benefit icons, reviews-forward, comparison, sticky CTA; fewer SKUs, more storytelling.
- **Card anatomy:** product + benefit tagline + rating + price; bundles/subscriptions.
- **Hero:** value-prop headline + hero product + CTA + trust row immediately.
- **Density:** medium; confident accent color, gradients allowed; rounded, friendly.
- **Type:** confident grotesk (Sora/Satoshi/Inter) bold headers.
- **Imagery:** product + benefit visuals, before/after, UGC.
- **Signature:** benefit icon row, review wall, sticky ATC, guarantee badge.
- **Study:** Allbirds, Ridge, Athletic Greens, Liquid Death.

## How to choose & differentiate (quick)

1. **Classify** the store: industry + personality (luxury / value / playful / technical /
   heritage), catalogue size, who buys and why.
2. **Reference**: open 2–3 best-in-class sites in that exact niche; note their archetype,
   card anatomy, hero, imagery, and one signature element worth adapting.
3. **Pick / blend** an archetype above → this is the SKELETON. If a past LitOS redesign in a
   different industry used the same bones, change them (grid ratio, hero type, section
   shapes, density, card anatomy, motif).
4. **Synthesise** one store-specific design language: tokens + ≥1 signature element unique to
   this brand/category. Record the chosen archetype + reference sites in
   `full_redesign_proposal.design_system`.
5. **Sanity check**: side-by-side with the last redesign, would a stranger see two different
   studios? If they look like siblings, differentiate harder.
